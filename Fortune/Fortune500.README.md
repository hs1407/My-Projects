# Fortune 500 Company Performance and Benefits Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes a Fortune 500–style company dataset to compare financial performance, workforce size, employee benefits, paid time off, maternity leave, and average employee tenure. The analysis explores whether companies can be evaluated not only by revenue, but also by the quality of workplace support they provide.

## Business Objective

The objective is to identify companies and industries that combine strong revenue performance with competitive employee benefits. The project provides a framework for comparing organizations from both a business-performance and employee-experience perspective.

## Business Problem

Revenue is often used as the primary measure of company success, but it does not show whether an organization supports and retains its employees. Job seekers, human-resources teams, business leaders, and industry researchers may need to compare financial strength with healthcare coverage, paid leave, parental support, and workforce stability.

The business challenge is to determine which companies offer the strongest overall balance rather than ranking them on revenue alone. This requires evaluating financial and employee-related measures together and identifying meaningful differences across industries.

## Dataset Overview

The dataset contains company-level information such as:

- Company name and industry
- Revenue
- Number of employees
- Availability of healthcare benefits
- Paid time off
- Maternity leave
- Average employee tenure

The data includes recognizable company names together with illustrative company records. It is intended for educational analysis and should not be treated as a current or authoritative Fortune 500 ranking.

## Analysis Performed

### Industry and Revenue Analysis

- Reviewed the overall company dataset
- Compared total revenue across industries
- Identified industries meeting a selected revenue benchmark
- Examined companies generating revenue above a defined level
- Evaluated the highest and average revenue represented in the dataset

### Employee Benefits Analysis

- Identified companies offering healthcare benefits
- Examined technology companies providing extended maternity leave
- Compared paid time off and maternity-leave policies across companies
- Counted organizations meeting both paid-leave and maternity-leave standards

### Company Comparison

- Grouped companies into revenue-based performance categories
- Identified companies that combine stronger revenue with competitive leave policies
- Created a more balanced comparison of financial performance and employee support

## Key Analytical Questions

The project is designed to answer questions such as:

- Which industries generate the greatest combined revenue?
- Which companies perform above the selected revenue benchmark?
- Which organizations provide healthcare benefits?
- Which companies offer both competitive paid time off and maternity leave?
- Which companies demonstrate the strongest balance between revenue and employee benefits?
- Can employee tenure help indicate whether workplace policies support retention?

## Recommendations

- Evaluate companies using a balanced scorecard that includes revenue, employee benefits, leave policies, and tenure instead of relying on revenue alone.
- Compare benefit policies within the same industry, because workforce expectations and operating models can vary significantly across sectors.
- Prioritize companies that show both strong financial performance and meaningful employee support when identifying potential employers or industry leaders.
- Encourage high-revenue companies with limited healthcare or leave benefits to reinvest in employee support and improve their competitiveness as employers.
- Use average tenure as a supporting retention indicator and investigate whether stronger benefits are associated with longer employee relationships.
- Add revenue per employee to future analysis so companies of different sizes can be compared more fairly.
- Expand the dataset with voluntary turnover, employee satisfaction, compensation, flexible-work options, and gender-neutral parental leave.
- Validate company information against current annual reports and official benefit policies before using the analysis for real employment or investment decisions.
- Refresh the data regularly because revenue, workforce size, and benefit policies can change over time.
